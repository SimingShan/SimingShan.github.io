水墨

The other resident of this apartment — a Siamese named after ink-wash painting, and the reason this site has a blog at all.

Her name is 水墨 — shuǐmò, the ink wash of Chinese brush painting. It is a fitting name for a seal-point Siamese. The body is the colour of unpainted rice paper; the face, the ears, the four paws and the tip of the tail are where the brush was loaded with ink and set down. Nobody planned the markings, and yet they look composed.

She came home small and has been quietly running the place ever since. There is a theory among cat people that the Siamese is less a breed than a personality that happens to arrive with a coat. 水墨 is evidence for it. She talks — not so much meows as opinions — follows me from room to room, and supervises everything I do, including, apparently, the writing of this paragraph.

水墨 has two settings. The first is a small loaf asleep in whatever spot I most recently wanted to use — the desk chair, the keyboard, the exact centre of a stack of papers. The second is a brief, total commitment to chaos, usually aimed at a catnip ball that has done nothing to deserve it.

She is, as far as I can tell, completely uninterested in full-waveform inversion. It keeps me humble.

Left: the catnip ball, moments before its ordeal. Right: the look she gives when she has decided dinner is late.

I don't have many hobbies. I do research, I look after 水墨, and every so often I write something down. This page exists mostly so that she has somewhere to live on the internet too. More updates — hers, and occasionally mine — will turn up here.
